You do not need to know anything about code to post a recipe. If someone has set up your blog
for you, everything happens in your web browser, on a computer, a tablet or a phone. This guide
walks through one recipe from start to finish.

> [!NOTE]
> Whoever set up your blog gives you two things: the address of your blog's editor (it ends in
> `/admin`) and your sign-in name. You choose the password the first time.

## 1. Sign in and start a new post

1. Open your blog's `/admin` address and sign in.
2. Choose **New post**.
3. Type the recipe's name as the title, for example *Grandma's apple cake*.

## 2. Tell the blog it is a recipe

1. Open **Settings**, at the top of the editor.
2. Find **Content profile** and choose **Recipe**.
3. New boxes appear. Fill in the ones you know:
   - **Servings**: a number, such as `8`. With a number here, readers can make the recipe for
     more or fewer people.
   - **Yield**: what it makes, such as `1 cake` or `24 cookies`, if that is clearer than servings.
   - **Prep time** and **Cook time**: written as you would say them, such as `20 min` or
     `1 hr 15 min`.
   - **Cuisine**, **Category** (Dessert, Dinner…) and **Calories** if you like.
4. Add a short **Description**. It is the sentence people see in search results and when the
   recipe is shared, so make it sound tasty.

## 3. Write the ingredients and steps

Go back to **Write**. It works like a word processor.

1. Write a few words about the recipe first, if you like: where it came from, who made it.
2. Type **Ingredients** on its own line. Then choose **Heading 2** from the style menu, so it
   becomes a heading.
3. Below it, start a **bulleted list**, with one ingredient on each line. **Start each line with
   the amount**, like `2 cups flour` or `1/2 tsp salt`. That is what lets readers scale it.
4. Type **Steps** on its own line, and make it a **Heading 2** as well.
5. Below it, start a **numbered list**, with one step on each line. Write times as words, such
   as "bake for 45 minutes". Each one becomes a timer readers can tap.

That is all the recipe needs. The ingredients must be a list under an *Ingredients* heading, and
the steps a list under a *Steps* heading. Plain bold text does not count, so use the heading
style.

> [!TIP]
> Anything after the steps, such as a **Notes** heading with storage tips or a family story, is
> fine. It appears as ordinary text after the recipe.

## 4. Add a photo

A photo makes a recipe, and search engines only show recipes that have one.

- On a computer, drag the photo into the editor, or use the picture button in the toolbar.
- On a phone or tablet, use the picture button and choose a photo from your library.

When asked, describe the photo in a few words, such as *a slice of apple cake on a blue plate*.
This is read aloud to people who cannot see the picture.

To make it the photo shown at the top and when the recipe is shared, choose it under **Cover
image** in **Settings**.

## 5. Check how it looks

Open **Site preview**. You see the page as readers will, with the recipe card, the servings
buttons, the checkboxes and the timers. Try them. If something looks wrong, go back to **Write**
and fix it. Nothing is public yet.

## 6. Publish

1. Choose **Save**.
2. When you are happy, choose **Publish** at the top of the editor. A minute or two later the
   recipe is on your blog.

If you want it to appear later, for example on Sunday morning, set the post to **Scheduled** with
a date and time instead. The blog publishes it by itself at that moment.

## Changing it later

Open the recipe from the list of posts, make your change, then **Save** and **Publish** again.
Nothing you do is lost: even a deleted post goes into a trash folder that can be recovered.

## Make the blog look like your kitchen

Under **Customize look** you can choose colours, fonts and text size by tapping, with a preview
before anything changes. **Landing page** arranges your home page: a welcome message, your latest
recipes, a favourite recipe at the top. [Making it look like yours](/posts/making-it-look-like-yours/)
explains both.
