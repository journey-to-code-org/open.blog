## Problem

The owners pulled up the living room carpet expecting plywood and found red oak instead. It was
grey with age, had two dark water stains by the radiator, and had a split board at the doorway
that caught socks.

## What we found

- The boards had about 4 mm of wood left above the tongue, enough for this sanding and at least
  two more in the future.
- The larger water stain was black, which means the stain had reached deep into the wood. That
  board would not sand clean.
- The split board had dried out next to a heating vent, not rotted. The subfloor under it was sound.

## What we did

1. Replaced the black-stained board and the split board with reclaimed red oak from a 1920s
   house in the next town, so the grain and colour would age to match.
2. Sanded in three passes, at 36, 60 and 100 grit, with dust-contained machines.
3. Filled the gaps between boards with natural filler and screened the whole floor smooth.
4. Applied two coats of water-based matte finish, with a light sanding between coats.

## Result

The floor is the colour of honey, and the two new boards are hard to find even when you know
where they are. The owners moved the furniture back in on the fourth day and are leaving the
rugs off for two weeks while the finish cures.

> [!TIP]
> If you find wood under your carpet, check a closet or a corner before you call someone. A nail
> and a flashlight at a gap between boards tells you a lot about how much wood is left.
