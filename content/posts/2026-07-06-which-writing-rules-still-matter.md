## The essay in brief

Orwell's 1946 essay argues that unclear writing and unclear thinking feed each other. It ends
with six rules: avoid worn-out figures of speech, use the short word, cut words you can cut,
prefer the active voice, avoid jargon when an everyday word exists, and break any of these rules
sooner than write something barbarous.

## Example Author: cut what you can cut

On the web, readers decide in seconds whether to stay. Every sentence that does not earn its
place is a reason to leave. Cutting words is the rule I can apply hardest and most often, because
it needs no taste, just a second read. If a paragraph still says the same thing without a
sentence, the sentence goes.

## Example Editor: the everyday word

Writing online is read by people in their second or third language, by translation tools, and
by screen readers at speed. Jargon fails all of them. The everyday word is the rule that widens
the audience most. And unlike the other rules, it is a kindness rather than a style.

## Agreeing to disagree

Both answers lean on the last rule. Neither rule is worth following if it makes the result worse.
